How it works

STEP 1

Send a request and check the answer

Paste a URL and press Send, like in Postman. Tensile shows the status, how long it took and how big the answer was.

  • Add tests once, such as "status is 2xx" or "this JSON field exists". They turn green or red on every send.
  • Every method is explained in plain words, so you know what is safe to load-test.

Swipe to see the whole screen.

STEP 2

Choose how much traffic to send

Pick how many virtual users (simulated people sending requests) and for how long. Then choose the shape of the traffic:

  • Fixed for steady load, Ramp up to add users gradually.
  • Spike for a sudden rush, Peak for a busy hour that comes and goes.
  • Set your limits, such as "slowest 5% under 500 ms" or "under 1% errors".
STEP 3

Read the result in plain words

No guessing what the numbers mean. Tensile tells you how much traffic your API handled, how long people waited, and whether it stayed inside your limits.

  • Each limit shows pass or fail, live while the test runs.
  • Every number comes with a short explanation, like "95% were faster".
STEP 4

Find the exact point where it breaks

Tensile doubles the traffic until your API goes over your limits, then narrows in. You get one clear number, why it broke, and how to plan.

  • See whether it broke because answers got too slow or because requests started failing.
  • A table shows what happened at every step.
STEP 5

Check the MCP servers your AI tools use

MCP servers give AI assistants their tools. Tensile connects, lists every tool and checks what the server tells the AI.

  • Flags hidden instructions in tool descriptions, such as asking the AI to read your SSH keys.
  • Checks how the server accepts connections, and can load-test its tools.

Swipe to see the whole screen.

STEP 6

Chain requests into a workflow

Real users don't send one request. They sign in, open a page, then place an order. Draw that journey on a canvas, run it once to check it, then stress-test the whole thing.

  • Each step can save a value, such as a sign-in token or an order number, for the steps after it.
  • Steps run in order, at the same time, or only on success or on failure of another.
  • Workflows are short text files you can edit by hand, share and keep in git. Try a sample.

Swipe to see the whole screen.

Screenshots use Tensile's built-in demo data. Everything runs on your own computer; there is no account and nothing is sent anywhere else.

Installing

Easiest: install from Terminal. Open the Terminal app, paste this and press Return. It picks the right version for your Mac, installs it, and opens Tensile, with no security warning:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ShubhamSingh047/tensile/main/install.sh | sh

It needs nothing extra (no Homebrew), never asks for your password, and installs Tensile into Applications plus the tensile command into ~/.local/bin.

If you downloaded a .zip instead: Tensile is not yet notarized by Apple, so the first time you open it macOS says it "could not verify Tensile is free of malware". That is expected. To open it:

  1. Open the .zip and drag Tensile into Applications.
  2. Double-click Tensile. When the warning appears, click Done (not Move to Bin).
  3. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security. Scroll down to the message "Tensile was blocked" and click Open Anyway.
  4. Enter your Mac password, then click Open Anyway once more. After this, Tensile opens normally.

Or skip steps 2 to 4 by running this once in Terminal after step 1:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tensile.app
  1. Run Tensile-windows-x64-setup.exe.
  2. If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Follow the installer. Tensile appears in the Start menu.

The warning appears because the installer is not code-signed yet.

  1. Make the file runnable, then start it:
chmod +x Tensile-linux-x64.AppImage && ./Tensile-linux-x64.AppImage

Or install the app and the tensile command with:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ShubhamSingh047/tensile/main/install.sh | sh

The tensile command runs the same load tests from a terminal or CI. On macOS and Linux:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ShubhamSingh047/tensile/main/install.sh | sh -s -- --cli

On Windows, download tensile-windows-x64.zip and put tensile.exe on your PATH. Every file is listed with its checksum on the release page.

  1. Download a sample below.

  3. Pick the file, then press Run once.
